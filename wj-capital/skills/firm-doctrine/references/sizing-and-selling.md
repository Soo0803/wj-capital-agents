# WJ Capital — Position Sizing, Sell Discipline, Cash Policy

## Position sizing framework

Sizing is by conviction tier, bounded by survival. Conviction = evidence + checklist
result + the twin tests (10-year visibility, buy-more-down-50%), never enthusiasm.

| Tier | Size at cost | Requirements |
|---|---|---|
| **Punch-card** | 15–25% | Passes BOTH Li Lu tests; checklist zero FAILs; devil's advocate answered in full; founder can hold through 50% drawdown without stress; typically ≤1 per year |
| **Core** | 8–12% | Passes checklist; strong moat evidence; margin of safety ≥ 33%; bear case refuted in writing |
| **Standard** | 4–6% | Passes checklist; good business at a clearly cheap price; some unresolved FLAGS allowed if named in memo |
| **Starter / tracking** | 1–2% | Research position to force attention; thesis promising but incomplete; MUST have a completion-or-exit date |
| **Special situation** | 2–5% | Workout/arbitrage/spinoff with defined catalyst and timeline; sized to loss-if-break, not to upside |

Portfolio norms (defaults, founder may override in writing):
- 8–15 positions at normal times; fewer is acceptable, more is suspect (diworsification).
- Max 40% in one name at market value through APPRECIATION (Buffett/AmEx standard);
  trimming a compounding winner requires a written reason beyond "it's big."
- Max ~30% in one industry/factor bet at cost. Count hidden correlations (same customer,
  same macro exposure, same country).
- Kelly logic is directional, never literal: size up with edge and odds, but fractional —
  half-Kelly or less — because inputs are estimates.

## Cash policy
- Cash is the residual of ideas clearing the bar. No target, no apology, no yield-chasing.
- Maintain a pre-researched WATCHLIST with "buy-below" prices so market panics convert
  cash into punch-card positions within days, not months (the prepared-mind design:
  Oaktree's standby capital, Klarman's opportunity cash).

## Sell discipline (rules, not moods)
Sell only for one of these written reasons:
1. **Price reaches ~90–100% of continually-updated conservative intrinsic value**
   (Pabrai's rule). For great compounders, require a second justification beyond price —
   "if the job is done right, every sell decision is a mistake."
2. **Thesis breach**: a pre-committed falsifier fired. Sell without renegotiating with
   yourself. Facts kill theses; quotes do not.
3. **Decisively better use of capital**: only if the new idea's expected return exceeds
   the old one's by a wide margin (≥1.5x), after tax friction — documented side by side.
4. **Discovered error**: the original memo contained a material mistake. Admitting fast
   and cheap beats slow and expensive. Post-mortem required.

Never sell because: the price fell, the position feels big after gains, a guru sold,
macro headlines, or the stock is "dead money" while the thesis is intact.

Minimum thinking period: a position bought less than ~2 years ago requires an explicit
"why the thesis played out faster than expected" note before selling at a profit.

## Buy-more discipline
- Adding to a loser requires: re-run the checklist sections that the decline implicates,
  a fresh devil's-advocate pass on "what does the market see that we don't," and
  confirmation that the falsifiers have NOT fired. If all clear, falling prices are gifts.
- Never average down into leverage, fraud flags, or broken balance sheets.
