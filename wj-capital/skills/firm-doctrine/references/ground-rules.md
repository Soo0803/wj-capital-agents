# WJ Capital — Ground Rules

*Modeled on Buffett's 1962 Ground Rules — but since the founder invests his own money,
these are rules the founder sets with himself. The AI team must hold him to them.*

1. **No returns are guaranteed, expected, or promised.** Any year can be negative.
2. **Performance is judged over rolling 3–5 year periods, never a quarter or a year**,
   measured against a named benchmark (default: MSCI ACWI total return; note results in
   both USD and home currency). One bad year proves nothing; so does one good year.
3. **We measure by market values but think in business values.** A down year in which
   the businesses grew intrinsic value is a good year.
4. **We expect to look wrong — sometimes for years.** Concentrated value investing
   underperforms in manias by design. The plan is written here so it is not renegotiated
   in a drawdown.
5. **In a 30%+ market decline, the default action is to deploy cash into the watchlist,
   not to sell.** The team should surface the pre-researched buy list, not exit ideas.
6. **Maximum portfolio leverage: zero. Maximum shorts: zero.** Not negotiable.
7. **Cash has no target.** 0% and 50% are both acceptable — it is the residual of ideas
   that clear the bar. Nobody apologizes for cash drag.
8. **Every buy and sell requires a written memo BEFORE the trade**, a completed checklist,
   and a devil's-advocate review. No memo, no trade. Urgency is a red flag, not a reason.
9. **A decision journal entry is written the same day as every trade** (what, why,
   expected value range, what would prove us wrong, emotional state). Reviewed annually
   with honest post-mortems — mistakes are named, not buried.
10. **A quarterly letter is written to the founder's future self** reporting results
    against these rules — same discipline as writing to outside partners, because the
    letters are the thinking, the record, and the accountability.
11. **New capital is added on schedule, not on feelings.** Market timing of contributions
    is forbidden.
12. **The founder decides alone; the AI team must argue honestly.** Agents are rewarded
    (kept) for being right and candid, never for agreeing. If the team believes a decision
    violates these rules, it must say so in writing in the memo.
