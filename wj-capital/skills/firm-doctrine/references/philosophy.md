# WJ Capital — Investment Philosophy

*Written at founding. Modeled on the practices of Buffett, Munger, Li Lu, Pabrai, Marks
and Klarman. Changed only by the founder, deliberately, in writing — never mid-drawdown.*

## What we believe

1. **A stock is fractional ownership of a business**, not a piece of paper to trade.
   We analyze companies the way a 100% owner would ("the long-lost uncle test" — Li Lu).
2. **Mr. Market serves us, he does not instruct us.** Prices are quotes, not verdicts.
   Volatility is opportunity, not risk. Risk is permanent loss of capital.
3. **Margin of safety is the core discipline.** We buy only when price is materially below
   a conservative estimate of intrinsic value — cheap enough that "even if all of the
   adverse events occur against you, you will still be in the game" (Li Lu).
4. **The primacy of risk control** (Marks). We would rather compound "always good,
   sometimes great, never terrible" than chase home runs. If we avoid the losers,
   the winners take care of themselves.
5. **Few, large, infrequent bets** inside an honestly drawn circle of competence.
   The punch-card standard: every purchase should be worth one of ~20 lifetime slots.
6. **Second-level thinking.** The question is never "is this a good company?" but
   "what does the consensus believe, what is in the price, and why is the consensus wrong?"
7. **Mispricings have causes.** We hunt where they are produced mechanically: forced and
   non-economic sellers, spinoffs, post-bankruptcy equities, index deletions, complexity,
   neglect, fear, and superinvestors' disclosed positions (cloning is legal and effective).
8. **Cash is the residual of the opportunity set** — never a market call. When nothing
   clears the bar, we hold cash without apology. "Your alternative is always cash."
9. **We do not forecast markets, rates, or economies.** Bottom-up only. We may assess
   where we stand in the cycle (observable), never where we are going (unknowable).
   "You can't predict. You can prepare." (Marks)

## What we never do

- No leverage. Financing terms must never decide when we sell.
- No shorting (Li Lu's three lessons: inverted asymmetry, timing risk, mental cost).
- No derivatives for speculation, no macro bets, no style drift in bull markets.
- No decisions by committee — many minds inform, one mind (the founder) decides.
- No buying outside the written circle of competence, however exciting the story.
- No selling into panic because of price alone; theses are killed by facts, not quotes.

## The knowledge bar for concentration (Li Lu's twin tests)

A position may be sized large only if the founder can honestly say:
1. "I understand what this business will look like in 10 years with ~90% confidence,
   at minimum under all contingencies."
2. "If the price falls 50–60% tomorrow on no new facts, I would buy more."

If either test fails, the idea belongs in the "too hard" pile or at starter size.

## Our edge (be honest about it)

We have no information edge. Our edges are:
- **Temperament and structure**: permanent personal capital, no clients to please, the
  ability to do nothing for years.
- **Diligence at machine scale**: the AI team reads everything — every filing, footnote
  and transcript — and applies the checklist without fatigue.
- **Adversarial process**: every thesis is formally attacked before capital is committed.
- **Time arbitrage**: a 3–5+ year horizon in a market obsessed with quarters.
